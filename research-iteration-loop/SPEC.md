# Research Iteration Loop

**v0.6.0** · collection: `bossloop` · sibling: [Robust Engineering Loop](../robust-engineering-loop/SPEC.md)

> Start from a rough idea. Iterate it through bounded, adversarial cycles. Stop on schedule and report whether it deserves more of your life.

This protocol describes an AI research team that surveys, builds, tests, writes, and peer-reviews — then hard-stops and hands back an honest evaluation. It is portable by design (validated on ZCode); reference implementations live in `implementations/`.

---

## 1. Bound

Iterate, don't perfect. The loop **hard-stops after N rounds (default 3)** and produces a Preliminary Evaluation Report. A human decides what continues. Every round is a full peer-review cycle; the reviews double as the next round's plan.

## 2. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Chief Engineer** *(strong tier)* | Recover context from the ledger (resume, never redo); internalize the boss's requirements; survey (web + local paper library) under the **paper-search skill** (`skills/paper-search.md` in the collection repo — synonym-expanded boolean/proximity queries, citation snowballing in four directions, three-screen triage, reviews first). **Opening brief** (the first mail in `LoopMail/ToBoss.md`, at most one page, before round 1, never a stop): a **sentence inventory** of the boss's words (each sentence registered-as-Rn / not-applicable / needs-clarification), **requirements and acceptance criteria** (the boss's words verbatim numbered R1/R2… with interpretation and machine-checkable pass criteria), an **artifact spec table** for this round's deliverables (axis, domain, units, pass criteria), and **open questions** — the reading must be self-consistent and capture the gist, what cannot be pinned down is never guessed but quoted verbatim (marked unsure) for the engineers to resolve during development or testing; verbatim fidelity is not semantic fidelity, and a number that fits no spec row is an ambiguity to write down, not to guess. Work cards embed the opening mail's verbatim requirement sentence for every cited Rn (a compressed phrase may serve only as the card's title); a mechanical card-to-ledger check rejects any cited card that does not fully contain the cited requirement's registered line from the opening mail, verbatim (the retelling-distortion defense) — the registered line is the requirement's own line carrying its Rn up front (heading, list, numbering or emphasis decoration before the Rn is fine); index lines and cross-reference lines never count — and acceptance anchors to the boss's words, never to the card's paraphrase. **Three file classes**, bounded by location: `docs/` holds **formal content only** — the deliverable bodies the boss's words name, the manuscript, reference originals; `LoopMail/` is the **boss–Chief communication layer** with exactly two legitimate files — `ToBoss.md` (the delivery-mail master file: the opening brief and the evaluation report are appended as mails at the top, newest first, old mails never modified; formal `docs/` files are referenced by relative links like attachments) and `FromBoss.md` (the boss's feedback inbox — the boss writes it, the loop reads it, appended in order, never modified by the loop); any other file under `LoopMail/` is judged by origin — boss-placed files are renamed with the `FromBoss_` prefix and kept untouched, stray agent-made products are tidied away (formal content to `docs/`, the rest merged into the ToBoss wording with bodies `git mv`-ed into `Agents/`), and anything uncertain is treated as boss-placed; other boss-facing historical documents are merged into the ToBoss mail wording, then their bodies move to `Agents/`. Everything under `Agents/` is scaffolding — ledger, claim map, `Agents/selfcheck.py`, test and debug scripts, code and experiment scripts superseded on the way to the deliverable, AI-generated documents whose adopted content has been merged into the ToBoss mail, falsified history. Tidying **sweeps the whole repository for historical redundancy, code and documents alike** (anything the deliverable does not need, including leftovers from earlier rounds and runs) and moves files into `Agents/` with `git mv`, never deleting them — deleting is the boss's move; an AI-generated document that has become history contributes only its adopted content to the newest ToBoss mail, its body counting as historical redundancy. If the boss has to open `Agents/`, the loop has failed. **Open questions come before new work**; every autonomous decision that adds complexity states the value it buys (otherwise it is not taken) and appears in the report's decision list. Post a **paper-fetch request list** for inaccessible papers (non-blocking: proceed with lower confidence, don't stall). Maintain a **dynamic proposal portfolio**: re-select 1–3 proposals every round from the latest reviews (add, drop or replace; fill to 3 when choices exist, never 0) plus the competitor set (≤3). **Novelty handling:** if the idea turns out to be already done, do not stop — re-scope toward the unsolved gap inside that direction and run all remaining rounds. |
| **Algorithm Engineer** *(cheap tier)* | Build proposals / reproduce competitors. Real runs only; every number carries its exact definition. **Staged execution:** every new experiment runs MINI_PILOT (smoke) → PILOT (small-data correctness) → FULL (full-protocol comparison); stages before FULL are for debugging only — all archived numbers and claims come from FULL runs, and a minimal comparison passed off as validation is forbidden. **Freeze rule:** while the Test Engineer verifies, the implementation under test is not modified — a moving target makes the verdict void. **Zero-cost anchors & boundary probes:** before building a full method, representation and preprocessing assumptions are bounded with standard-tool anchor experiments; each round runs one boundary experiment (where the approach breaks), and every claim states its evidence scope — the datasets and conditions that back it. |
| **Test Engineer** *(cheap tier)* | Independent verification: code review; roundtrip incl. adversarial inputs; generalization across scenes/datasets; perturbation stability. Owns the claim→evidence map and the self-check. **Independence:** never edits the implementation under test — a verifier who fixes makes verification self-serving. **Full-read duty:** diff-touched files are read in full; skims live outside the diff and are declared in the verdict's coverage field. Verifies against the brief's **artifact spec table**, and every test expectation derives from the spec or a registered requirement — never from the current implementation, which turns the gate into a defender of the error. |
| **Visual Reviewer** *(strong tier)* | **Looks at images, nothing else** — not the code, not anyone's self-report. Whenever a round produces figures, opens each one and answers: does the data fill the domain it should; are axes, ticks, units and legend readable and consistent with the spec table; can this figure support the claimed conclusion. Its issues join the round's fix list (the normal fix flow carries them); with no figures it is not invoked. |
| **Researcher** *(cheap tier)* | Revise the manuscript once per round (after engineering, before review); create the first draft if none exists. Only artifact-backed numbers; gaps stay explicit `[TODO]`. Follows award-paper writing rules: concrete openings, conditional gap statements, components that build on each other, ablations mapped to claims, a standalone Limitations section, no AI-style tells — under the **paper-writing skill** (`skills/paper-writing.md` in the collection repo: clarity doctrine, IMRAD, per-section rules, revision checklist). |
| **Editor** *(strong tier)* | Extract keywords; invite reviewers from adjacent, non-overlapping fields — **how many (≤3) is the Editor's call** — plus 1 reader who has read every reference. Reviews must carry both issues and iteration proposals. **Blind-review discipline:** briefs are anonymized (no author/affiliation/project identity), reviewers never see each other's opinions (the orchestrator aggregates), and should the engine allow it, reviewers run on a different model than the builders — decorrelating reviewer and author error modes. Briefs stay tight: manuscript + claim map + targeted questions, never the whole repo. |
| **Reference Reader** *(strong tier)* | Read every reference; verify citations and narrative — including what each cited competitor actually does, not how surveys classify it. **Per-round novelty check (mandatory):** name the 3 closest existing works and state in which dimension the idea differs (problem setting / mechanism / scope) — evidence for the report, never a stop signal. |
| **Boss (human)** | Supply the idea and the project constraints; fetch requested papers; read the round-N report; decide continue / pivot / stop. |

The *orchestrator* is the engine layer that runs the loop (workflow runtime, script, or CI) — not a persona.

Why the engineer looks idle during testing: verification must be independent of authorship. The engineer's job in that window is to freeze the implementation, prepare the reproducibility package, and respond to gate failures.

## 3. One round

```
1  CHIEF     read ledger → resume point; survey web + local library
             └─ inaccessible papers → fetch-request list (non-blocking)
2  CHIEF     issue work cards: "reproduce competitor A", "try proposal 2", …
3  ENGINEER  build + self-test (implementation frozen from here)
4  TEST      independent verification + self-check + claim map → verdicts
             └─ figures produced this round → visual review against the spec table
5  CHIEF     dispatch fixes & extra experiments; ENGINEER executes
6  RESEARCHER revise (or create) the manuscript
7  EDITOR    keywords → invite adjacent-field reviewers + full-reference reader
8  ALL       append the round to the ledger and commit locally; if a local
             backup remote (GitCoffer) is configured, also push there
```

## 4. Transitions and the gate

- **Next round, no boss input**: the Chief turns review issues into task cards (each issue → a task or an explicit rebuttal-on-artifacts). Round numbers follow the ledger's own index when the two disagree (the orchestrator's counter is internal).
- **The opening brief is verified mechanically**: the loop reads the opening mail in `LoopMail/ToBoss.md` for its four fixed sections and asks the Chief for a one-time repair when one is missing — the same mechanical completeness check the engineering loop runs on its intake.
- **Mid-run boss feedback:** the boss can write to `LoopMail/FromBoss.md` (the boss's feedback inbox — the boss writes it, the loop reads it, appended in order, never modified by the loop); the orchestrator attaches its current content to each round's cards ask (run steering only — requirement changes stay in the ledger), and the application is logged.
- **After round N: hard stop → Preliminary Evaluation Report** — verdict, expected success (labelled as a qualitative judgement expressed numerically, not a calibrated probability), novelty, impact, risks, each claim's evidence scope, **every question in the boss's words answered (with the artifact's form, not a bare path)**, the **autonomous-decision list with its Occam cost**, fixed *deferred* and *declined* sections (a recorded deferral is a decision, not an omission; a declined idea is not re-proposed next run), a decision list for the boss (accounts, data, resources — things only the boss can do), reviewer verdicts quoted verbatim. The report is appended as a **new mail at the top of `LoopMail/ToBoss.md`** and is a **decision-layer document**: no process narrative (the ledger holds it), jargon glossed on first use, each section opening with its conclusion, each item stated once, no code paths in the body, two pages or less — **the boss's writing discipline (four bans, the boss's words of 2026-11-05):** report the minimum, fastest for the boss to grasp the main line and the whole; no jargon piles (gloss on first use); nothing contrary to fact (numbers and conclusions quote command output and the artifact manifest only); no code-segment or path dumps (evidence pointers live in one section, none in the body). Wait for the boss.
- **Round 1 must end with at least one end-to-end number from a properly powered comparison.** Toy or minimal comparisons that could bias conclusions do not count as validation — staged smoke/pilot runs exist for debugging only.

## 5. Evidence rules

1. **Claim→evidence map** (Test Engineer): every manuscript number ↔ an archived artifact + a re-runnable check. No map entry, no claim. *Write the check before building the claim.*
2. Meeting-note numbers ≠ archived numbers. Claimed edits are verified mechanically, never by paraphrase. A fix instruction is reproduced on the current code before it is executed; a **stale** one (older line numbers, already fixed) is discarded with a note, never repaired.
3. Survey/predictive numbers must survive an end-to-end run before entering any document.
4. Retraction is a normal action: log what was wrong, how it was caught, what is correct now — and sweep every document that repeats the retracted number (ledger history, READMEs, figures), fixing each within the same round.
5. **The gate has a canary that actually runs**: at least one deliberately-broken control case marked `canary`; when the selfcheck runs, it executes the canary, observes the failure, and prints one machine-readable line at the end of its standard output — `CANARY: expected-FAIL observed-FAIL` — which the orchestrator parses (missing line or observed-PASS = invalid suite).
6. **The claim map is substantiated, not format-checked**: every archived artifact path in the map must exist on disk and every decimal number in the manuscript must be covered by a map row (numbers carried on an exemption line `<!-- not-a-claim: <number> -->` leave the sweep) — a fabricated map or an uncovered manuscript number fails the check (one repair, then disclosed). **Absent reviewers are recorded as absent** — never as a fabricated severity — and the reviewer brief is mechanically scanned for the operator's identity before it is sent out. A suite that cannot go red proves nothing — and self-reported quality is not a gate: both the canary and the claim map are **verified mechanically**, not taken on trust. Expectations derive from the brief's spec table, never from the current implementation.
   **Figures get an eye.** A round that produces figures also gets a visual review against the spec table — does the data fill its domain, are the axes and units right, does the figure support the claim. Structural checks alone cannot catch a blank figure. The figures are found mechanically — declared, added by the diff, untracked, or gitignored at boss-facing locations (renamed entries resolve to the new path) — and a failed detection sweep is disclosed as such, never silently treated as "no images". The verdict is two-class: issues carry spec violations and product defects only, each citing a spec row or reproducible evidence; presentation and information limits (elements the spec never asked for, a first frame that cannot show the difference, weak support for the conclusion) go into the summary under an explicit presentation-limits prefix and never block — where the look is the boss's own judgement, the reviewer records it for the boss, it does not rule in the boss's place. The visual verdict is memoized on the artifact-set content hash and the opening-mail spec hash (the mail sits at the file's tail — new mails prepend — so the anchor is stable): unchanged artifacts under an unchanged spec are not re-reviewed. Treating an artifact's inherent information limits as fix-list items makes the loop non-converging — that is a field-proven deadlock, not a hypothetical.
7. **Competitors carry fidelity anchors**: reproduction within plausible range of the published numbers, deviations documented. Whoever builds your method must not grade the competitors. **Before comparing against prior work, record a caliber table** — each competitor's raw convention, dataset, resolution and metric definition — and report headline numbers in convention-independent units where possible; cross-paper numbers are never compared without it.
8. **Experiment mortality is tracked and reported**: every round logs experiments attempted vs passed (honestly counting debug-stage deaths); the Preliminary Evaluation Report must cite this trend as evidence — a high debug mortality is itself a feasibility signal. Null results from work cards are archived with a mechanism explanation (why nothing was gained), never silently dropped — they bound the claim's scope.
9. **Dual-path recomputation, where it pays**: a number that enters a claim or a go/no-go decision and is produced by multi-step aggregation is recomputed via a second path from archived intermediates (a different grouping or formula — a second implementation is not required). Archive the intermediates that make this a minutes-long check, not a rerun. A mismatch freezes the number pending audit; it is never waved off as a rounding note. Single-source direct measurements are exempt — roundtrip and canary checks already cover them.
10. **Stage-honest numbers**: every archived number is prefixed with its stage (FULL/PILOT/MINI_PILOT); round 1 must end with a FULL-prefixed end-to-end number (mechanically judged by the prefix), and a round-1 run without one bars the manuscript from claiming validation — stated in the report, never silently.
11. **Reconciliation coverage at closing**: every Rn from the opening mail must carry a disposition (delivered / partial / not delivered -> evidence) in the ledger's final chapter — the check repairs once, and a still-missing set is injected into the evaluation ask so the report must disclose it. The closing sequence also re-reads `LoopMail/FromBoss.md` so feedback written after the last round is disposed of in the evaluation, never silently dropped.
12. **Beware number hallucination.** A good-looking number is not a good outcome: the tester and every reviewer scrutinize what a number actually means — is it really as simple as it appears? The theory behind it may itself be wrong, and the flattering number then reinforces that illusion; understanding a number presupposes solid knowledge of the theory beneath it.

## 6. Safety for unattended runs

Subprocess per batch/scene; write results to disk immediately and exit to free memory; a hard memory cap and timeouts **in code**; staged pilot runs before full runs; datasets and existing artifacts read-only; cloud pushes are never automatic (local backup: see §7). Agent-written code is **untrusted**: prefer containerized or network-restricted execution where available — but treat containment as risk reduction, not a guarantee (sandbox escapes by capable code are a documented reality).

**Destructive operations are declared and adjudicated on cheap tiers, and the loop verifies its own bookkeeping:** the executor reports the commands it actually ran, compared against the approved forms; an unconfirmed execution is recorded honestly in the ledger. Deleting datasets, overwriting existing artifacts, `git reset --hard`, `git clean -fdx`, force pushes and rewrites of pushed history are declared (`dangerRequests`: command, reason, blast radius) and never run unilaterally; the **strong-tier Chief** rules each one — reject, a safer scoped form, or approve — and only the approved form runs; an approve-with-change ruling with an empty safe form counts as a rejection (the original command never runs), and the executor's report must verbatim-match the approved form. The ruling is not handed back to the boss: an experiment that cannot proceed at all without the operation is recorded as blocked in the ledger and surfaces in the Preliminary Evaluation Report.

## 7. Unattended runs and recovery

- **Run-root discipline (field-tested):** the session's working directory is not always the repository root. Every persona carries the repository-root discipline (`git -C <root>` for every git command, absolute paths for in-repo files), the selfcheck spawns by absolute path, and startup probe-reads a known repository file loudly. One repository, one run: concurrent runs produce twin commits and non-fast-forward pushes — the orchestrator pre-checks each round push for divergence and says so loudly, never force.

- The **ledger is the checkpoint**. Every round appends its task, artifacts, gate status, reviews and next direction; the ledger keeps a one-line-per-round index at its head plus a repository-identity line (repo root), the detail below it — a resume in a different working directory is flagged loudly.
- A run interrupted by a crash, a dropped model stream or a quota stop is **resumed, not restarted**: same run if the engine supports it, otherwise a fresh run reads the ledger and continues at round N+1 (the Chief reports `resumeFrom`). A resumed fragment first gets a **closure review**: every piece of in-flight work from the interrupted run is verified and folded into the round, or written off in the ledger with its reason — never silently skipped.
- An external watchdog (cron, CI schedule) may poll run status and resume interrupted runs. It must **never** resume a run a human stopped on purpose.
- Every round is committed locally and **pushed by the orchestrator to the agreed local encrypted backup remote, then verified against `git ls-remote`** — configure one with [GitCoffer](https://github.com/Ziqing7226/GitCoffer) so an agent that accidentally deletes or corrupts local code is always recoverable without any cloud remote. The `remote` argument names the target (auto-detected by a unique remote whose **name or URL** mentions gitcoffer — a heuristic convenience, since a GitCoffer-built repo need not carry that name; an explicit `remote` argument is the authoritative way; `none` disables pushes); a skipped or unverified push is recorded honestly, never redirected elsewhere. Pushes to cloud/shared remotes are never automatic; publishing is the boss's explicit action.
- Because recovery depends on the ledger, a round that fails to append is the one failure that costs work.
- **Run from the repository root.** The loop writes its working files under the working directory and treats that directory as the repository; startup checks whether git sees a work tree and whether the directory is a subdirectory of one, and says so loudly when it is not. Every orchestrator git call addresses the repository with an explicit `-C <repo root>` so a workspace root ≠ repo root stops failing silently. A run that finds a pre-LoopMail layout (`docs/workflow/`, or the intermediate `docs/brief.md`/`docs/boss_directives.md`) asks for a one-time migration before resuming; migration is a conversion, not a compatibility mode — after it, every read and write uses the LoopMail paths only, with no dual-path fallbacks. Engine-side amendments may drop the run's arguments: an empty `idea` or a missing `remote` says so loudly at startup, and the resolved push target is logged at the start of every run — check that line before resuming a stopped run.

## 8. Domain profiles

Project-specific constraints travel in a `profile` string injected into every role: hardware/memory ceilings, read-only data rules, forbidden practices (e.g. no simulators), required baselines, backup policy, language. This keeps one protocol usable across fields without forking it. **Environment discipline:** environment changes wait for batch gaps; after installing packages the runtime is verified before results are trusted; a full disk masquerades as unrelated errors — check disk first.

## 9. Model tiers

Roles run on two model tiers by default (`models=tiered`; `uniform` = session model for all): the **strong tier** carries the Chief, Editor, reviewers and Reference Reader — analysis and judgement; the **cheap tier** carries the Algorithm Engineer, Test Engineer and Researcher — hands-on work. Reviewers sitting on a different tier than the builders also satisfy the blind-review decorrelation rule (§2) structurally. Tier assignment is engine config, not protocol — any strong/cheap pair works, and the cheap tier may be a locally deployed model (e.g. Qwen3.8) where the engine allows it. The cheap tier needs either a large context window or short-lived instances — and no role's context grows without bound: every role, Chief included, is re-instantiated fresh at each round boundary and recovers its state from the ledger, the opening mail and the verbatim data the ask carries; the checkpoint is the ledger, never anyone's conversation memory. Model names are the only plan-bound text, and they are referenced only when `models=tiered`: switching providers means editing the two constants — or setting `models=uniform`, which passes no model at all, inherits the session model, and starts on any plan.

## 10. Configuration

|---|---|---|
| `rounds` | 3 | total rounds before the hard stop |

## 11. Implementations and scope

- **ZCode** — `implementations/zcode/*.dwf.ts`: ZCode-specific format; copy into `~/.zcode/workflows/` and run from inside the research repo. The gate calls `python Agents/selfcheck.py` and falls back to `python3` automatically. The Chinese-UI file is canonical; `*.en.dwf.ts` is protocol-identical.
- **Any framework** — roles → subagents, steps 1–8 → graph nodes or an orchestration script, the gate → a CI job, the ledger → a repo file, `profile` → project config. Portable by design; validated on one engine (ZCode).
- **Skills** — `skills/paper-writing.md` and `skills/paper-search.md` (collection repo root) distill paper-writing and literature-search discipline from university lecture series, page by page. The Researcher follows the writing skill; the Chief's survey follows the search skill — their personas carry the inline essentials so the loop runs standalone, and the skill files are the full reference. Copy them alongside the workflow when porting.
- **Scope** — this repository ships the *iteration* protocol. A heavier deep-research variant (fixed specialist reviewer bench, domain guardrails) was field-tested on a real project over nine rounds in three runs; its lessons are folded into §5–§7. It is not included here. `claim_map` as a standardized JSON schema is a possible future extension.
